"This is Anna. She needs to charge her car this afternoon. Let me walk you through exactly what happens."

  1. Book — Open the booking page, pick a station and a charger, choose 60 minutes.

    "She picks a station and how long she needs. Watch what happens when I change the length — the available start times change with it. That is not a stored list of slots; it is worked out from what is genuinely free."

  2. Pay the deposit — Complete the simulated payment.

    "A small deposit confirms it. She has ten minutes to pay before the charger is released to someone else. I should say clearly — this payment is simulated. No card details are ever entered, stored or shown anywhere."

  3. QR code appears — Show the confirmation screen.

    "And here is her QR code. This is what she shows when she arrives."

  4. Operator checks her in — Abdel Aziz types the code and checks in.

    "Over to the station side."

  5. Charging starts, then ends — Abdel Aziz starts, then ends.

    "She plugs in and charges. And here is something worth watching — she finishes early. That leftover time does not just disappear. It goes straight back on sale, and within a minute the system offers it to somebody who was waiting."

  6. A charger breaks — Abdel Aziz reports a fault.

    "Now something goes wrong. A charger fails. The system works out which bookings are affected and how far the delay spreads down the queue — and it tells those drivers rather than letting them find out at the station."

  7. Extension request — Request more time from the driver side.

    "Anna asks for more time. The system checks whether it genuinely fits before the next booking. If it fits, she gets it. If only part of it fits, she gets that part and is told why. And if the next customer would be affected, she is refused — because that customer's booking is a promise too."

  8. Notifications — Open the bell.

    "And Anna has been told about all of it — the offer, the delay, the decision on her extra time. These messages are not written by the booking code. They are produced separately by reading the record of what happened, which means a failure to send a message can never cause a booking to fail."

  9. Analytics update — Open /admin/schedule-quality.

    "And every number here has moved. Thirty-one measurements — how full the chargers are, how often drivers get the time they wanted, how long people wait, how many waitlisted drivers we eventually serve."

    Point at a sample size: "Notice each one shows how many cases it is based on. And where we have no data, it says 'no data' rather than showing zero — because zero and 'we don't know' are very different things."

If something does not respond: the background services run once a minute. Say "this runs on a timer, so give it a moment" and move to the next step — do not stand in silence waiting. Come back to it.

Slide 14 · Results 0:45

"How do we know it works? One hundred and eighty-two automatic checks, and they run every time we change anything."

"They do not check that the code runs. They check things like: two bookings that overlap — the second one is refused. Two bookings back to back — both accepted. A held charger cannot be taken by anyone else. And leaving early really does put the time back on sale."

Questions likely to come to you Q&A

"Did you design this yourself?" — Yes. One set of colours, fonts and components used across every screen, with a minimum touch size so it works on a phone.

"Does it work on mobile?" — Yes, every screen is built for small screens first.

"Why does that say 'no data'?" — Because there genuinely is none for that period. We deliberately never show zero in place of missing data — a zero looks like a real measurement and would be misleading.

"What is the countdown on the offer?" — The charger is really held for five minutes. The number comes from the server, not the phone's clock, so a device with the wrong time cannot show a hold that has already expired as still valid.

"Can I see the deposit figures?" — Be honest: the data is all recorded, but we have not built a screen for it yet. It is on our list.
